# Research and Evidence Method

## Objective

Build a practical evidence foundation for a developmental human-understanding assessment without confusing expert lectures, popular theories, proprietary case studies, or perceived accuracy with formal validation.

## Source hierarchy

Use this order when evaluating a claim:

1. systematic reviews and meta-analyses
2. original peer-reviewed studies
3. professional testing standards and official guidance
4. technical manuals for validated instruments
5. university texts and academic books
6. long-form expert courses and lectures
7. secondary summaries
8. popular media and promotional claims
9. founder experience and proprietary case studies

Founder case studies are valuable hypothesis-generating evidence. They are not substitutes for controlled comparison.

## Evidence labels

Tag every claim with one of these labels:

### A — Strong
Supported by high-quality converging evidence, robust meta-analysis, or repeated findings with acceptable methods.

### B — Substantial
Supported by multiple credible studies but limited by population, measurement, effect size, or replication concerns.

### C — Promising
Plausible and supported by early or mixed evidence; useful for pilot testing but not strong claims.

### D — Contested or theoretical
Actively debated, conceptually valuable, or difficult to operationalize.

### P — Proprietary hypothesis
Derived from the project’s name/number method or case studies and not independently established.

### U — Unsupported
No credible evidence identified, or evidence meaningfully contradicts the claim.

## Required research record

For every source, capture:

- source ID
- citation
- source type
- DOI or stable location
- construct
- population and sample
- methods
- key result
- effect size where available
- limitations
- replication status
- cultural/developmental limits
- product relevance
- item-writing relevance
- interpretation relevance
- safety relevance
- evidence grade
- whether it supports, narrows, or contradicts a proprietary hypothesis

## Psychometric foundation to research

Codex or a research agent must study and summarize practical implications of:

- construct definition and operationalization
- classical test theory
- introductory item response theory
- item difficulty and discrimination
- internal consistency
- omega and alpha
- test–retest reliability
- observer agreement and inter-rater reliability
- content validity
- convergent and discriminant validity
- criterion and predictive validity
- incremental validity
- exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis
- measurement invariance
- differential item functioning
- acquiescence and extreme-response bias
- social-desirability and impression-management bias
- careless responding
- reverse-keyed item problems
- reading level and accessibility
- norm-referenced versus person-centered interpretation
- confidence intervals and uncertainty
- responsible feedback

Each summary must answer: **What does this change in our software, item bank, scoring, report, claim language, or pilot design?**

## Research-to-product filter

A concept belongs in V1 only when it passes most of these tests:

1. It explains a meaningful individual difference.
2. It can be measured through behaviorally clear questions.
3. It changes the guidance or interpretation.
4. It is not redundant with an existing construct.
5. It can be assessed without an excessively long quiz.
6. It can be discussed safely without diagnosis.
7. It is relevant to more than one assessment mode.
8. It can be tested against chart hypotheses.
9. Users can recognize or correct the conclusion.
10. The construct has sufficient research or a clearly labeled pilot rationale.

Interesting subjects that fail this filter belong in the research backlog, not the core quiz.

## Established frameworks to benchmark

Research these as measurement references, not as final product identities:

- Big Five and IPIP item pools
- HEXACO
- interpersonal circumplex models
- self-determination theory
- attachment measures
- behavioral inhibition and activation
- regulatory focus
- intolerance of uncertainty
- rejection sensitivity
- emotion regulation
- self-control and impulsivity
- psychological flexibility
- values and meaning
- locus of control and self-efficacy
- conflict style and communication research
- social status, prestige, and dominance
- empathy and perspective taking
- perfectionism

For every framework, document licensing. Do not reproduce copyrighted items without permission. Public-domain items may be adapted, but adaptations must be labeled and tested.

## Instructor and course method

Expert courses are research maps, not final authorities.

For every important instructor claim:

1. Record the claim accurately.
2. Find the underlying paper, book chapter, or evidence base.
3. Identify major competing explanations.
4. Check whether the effect is replicated.
5. Check whether the claim generalizes by age, culture, and context.
6. Decide whether it can become a construct, item, modifier, interpretation caution, or background concept.
7. Keep ideological and philosophical claims separate from empirical claims.

## Item-development workflow

1. Define the construct and facets.
2. Define observable behaviors at low, middle, and high expression.
3. Define normal and stress expression.
4. Write a large initial pool.
5. Remove double-barreled, moralized, vague, leading, or diagnostic items.
6. Conduct expert review.
7. Conduct cognitive interviews with target users.
8. Pilot items.
9. Analyze missingness, response distributions, discrimination, redundancy, and factor behavior.
10. Revise or remove weak items.

Do not create final interpretations before the item behavior is understood.

## Validation ladder

### Stage 0 — Content foundation
Construct definitions, evidence mapping, item review, licensing review, and safety review.

### Stage 1 — Usability pilot
Completion rate, comprehension, drop-off, time, perceived clarity, emotional response, and qualitative corrections.

### Stage 2 — Reliability pilot
Internal consistency, test–retest stability, observer agreement, and response-quality analysis.

### Stage 3 — Construct validation
Convergence with established measures, discrimination from adjacent constructs, and internal structure.

### Stage 4 — Incremental-value testing
Compare questionnaire-only, chart-only, and combined reports.

### Stage 5 — Practical outcome testing
Test whether recommendations improve communication, conflict repair, parenting behavior, or self-observation.

Only claims supported at the appropriate stage may appear in product marketing.

## Required comparison conditions

At minimum compare:

- questionnaire only
- chart only
- combined chart plus questionnaire
- generic flattering report
- deterministic structured report
- AI-enhanced narrative

This helps answer what actually creates value.

## Negative evidence policy

The project must record:

- chart hypotheses not supported
- items that fail
- constructs that do not separate clearly
- recommendations users report as unhelpful
- groups for whom the assessment behaves differently
- visually compelling scores that lack stable measurement

A result that cannot fail cannot improve.

## Research output format

Research agents should package conclusions as implementation-ready artifacts:

- construct records
- item metadata
- scoring hypotheses
- interpretation rules
- safety constraints
- visualization implications
- validation tests
- unresolved questions

Do not return only essays or lists of readings.
