# Results and Visualization Specification

## Purpose

The result experience must feel professionally deep while remaining honest, understandable, and actionable.

Visualizations are not decoration. Each graph must answer a specific human question and remain traceable to structured evidence.

## Visual integrity rules

1. Never imply clinical precision.
2. Do not display a percentage merely because a score can be normalized to 0–100.
3. Use ranges, bands, and confidence indicators when uncertainty is meaningful.
4. Clearly distinguish behavioral evidence from proprietary chart hypotheses.
5. Do not hide contradictions by averaging them away.
6. Never use one chart type for every result.
7. Every chart requires a text equivalent for accessibility.
8. Color must not be the only carrier of meaning.
9. Avoid red/green “bad/good” framing.
10. Labels must describe patterns, not moral value.
11. Graphs should remain readable on a phone.
12. Every score must link to the questions, rules, version, and evidence that produced it.

## Result-page hierarchy

### Layer 1 — Immediate identity recognition

Within the first screenful, show:

- a concise title describing the dominant pattern configuration
- a 2–4 sentence synthesis
- three strongest supported patterns
- one meaningful internal tension
- one practical takeaway

Example title:

> **The Independent Connector**

Example summary:

> You appear to need both meaningful connection and freedom over how you act. You are often cooperative when your choices remain intact, but pressure can make you withdraw or become unexpectedly forceful. The key pattern is not avoidance of people; it is protection of agency inside relationships.

Titles must be generated from versioned rules, not improvised mystical archetypes.

### Layer 2 — Visual pattern overview

Show the Human Pattern Wheel and Baseline vs Stress view.

### Layer 3 — Deep modules

Motivation, communication, conflict, sensitivities, contradictions, chart alignment, and growth leverage.

### Layer 4 — Practical actions

Shareable guidance, scripts, and environmental recommendations.

### Layer 5 — Evidence and correction

Confidence, source agreement, limitations, and feedback.

## Graph 1 — Human Pattern Wheel

### Question answered

What are the person’s strongest overall pattern dimensions?

### Recommended form

A circular or radial profile with 8–12 axes, but not a deceptive filled radar polygon by default. Prefer:

- radial bars
- segmented ring
- circular dot plot

Radar charts may exaggerate area differences and imply that unrelated axes form a geometric whole. If used, provide an accessible linear alternative.

### Data

Show construct expression bands for selected headline constructs:

- social engagement
- sensitivity
- regulation
- autonomy
- belonging
- certainty need
- adaptability
- directness/conflict

### Presentation

- low, moderate, and high expression bands
- confidence marker on each axis
- tap/keyboard focus reveals definition and evidence
- no good/bad direction
- text summary below

### Required interaction

Selecting a dimension opens:

- what the dimension means
- baseline expression
- stress expression
- supporting evidence
- possible misinterpretation
- practical implication

## Graph 2 — Baseline vs Stress Profile

### Question answered

How does the person change when pressure rises?

### Recommended form

A paired horizontal dot plot or slope chart.

For each priority construct, show:

- baseline position
- stress position
- direction and size of change
- confidence band

Examples:

- communication: open → withdrawn
- boundaries: flexible → rigid
- decision speed: deliberate → impulsive
- reassurance need: moderate → high
- social engagement: high → low

### Why it matters

This is likely one of the most valuable differentiators. Most quizzes describe one averaged personality. This graph shows state-dependent expression.

### Interpretation rule

Only visualize a stress shift when minimum evidence exists for both baseline and stress conditions.

## Graph 3 — Motivation Hierarchy

### Question answered

What most reliably activates effort and what tends to shut it down?

### Recommended form

Ordered horizontal bars or ranked cards.

Potential motives:

- autonomy
- mastery
- belonging
- recognition
- security
- meaning
- novelty
- contribution
- competition

Do not imply the ninth motive is absent. Show evidence strength and context.

### Additional display

Pair each top motive with:

- “activated by”
- “undermined by”
- “how to encourage”

Example:

> **Autonomy**
> Activated by clear outcomes with freedom over method.
> Undermined by micromanagement or unexplained rules.

## Graph 4 — Communication Profile

### Question answered

How does the person naturally communicate, process, and receive information?

### Recommended form

A multi-axis continuum dashboard rather than one score.

Possible continua:

- direct ↔ indirect
- immediate processing ↔ needs time
- verbal processing ↔ private processing
- detail-first ↔ big-picture-first
- emotionally explicit ↔ emotionally contained
- advice-seeking ↔ autonomy-protective

### Practical card

Below the graph show:

- best way to begin a difficult conversation
- best feedback format
- signs they are no longer processing well
- common misunderstanding
- repair phrase

## Graph 5 — Conflict and Boundary Map

### Question answered

What sequence tends to occur when tension rises?

### Recommended form

A process map or stepped flow, not merely a score.

Example:

```text
Notices tension
→ minimizes or delays
→ gathers internal evidence
→ feels choices narrowing
→ becomes unusually direct
→ needs separation before repair
```

Show alternative branches when evidence indicates context dependence.

Include:

- early trigger
- first visible behavior
- hidden need
- escalation risk
- helpful response
- unhelpful response
- repair window

## Graph 6 — Needs and Sensitivities Balance

### Question answered

Which needs are strongest, and which sensitivities make those needs easier to disrupt?

### Recommended form

Two aligned panels:

- core needs
- primary sensitivities

Potential needs:

- choice
- connection
- clarity
- recognition
- stability
- privacy
- competence

Potential sensitivities:

- rejection
- criticism
- control
- unpredictability
- public embarrassment
- being ignored
- emotional overload

Use connecting lines only when a coded rule links a need and sensitivity.

Example:

> Strong need for competence + high public-evaluation sensitivity → may avoid asking questions when others are watching.

## Graph 7 — Pattern Interaction Map

### Question answered

Which trait combinations create the person’s most important strengths and tensions?

### Recommended form

A small network diagram or interaction cards. Do not create a dense “brain map.” Limit to 3–5 high-value interactions.

Examples:

- autonomy × belonging
- sensitivity × regulation
- certainty × adaptability
- social engagement × recovery need
- directness × rejection sensitivity

For each interaction show:

- combined pattern
- strength expression
- friction expression
- conditions that shift it
- action lever

### Example

> **Autonomy + Belonging**
> Strength: loyal connection without losing individuality.
> Friction: reassurance may feel necessary, while advice may feel controlling.
> Leverage: ask for emotional support separately from decision guidance.

## Graph 8 — Chart vs Behavior Alignment

### Question answered

Which proprietary hypotheses were supported by lived evidence?

### Recommended form

A matrix or grouped evidence table.

Rows: chart hypotheses.

Columns:

- chart prediction
- behavioral support
- context support
- contradiction
- final classification
- confidence

User-facing classifications:

- supported
- partly supported
- context-dependent
- not supported
- unresolved

This graph is central to intellectual honesty.

Do not bury unsupported hypotheses.

## Graph 9 — Evidence and Confidence Panel

### Question answered

How much evidence supports each major conclusion?

### Recommended form

Stacked evidence indicators or confidence cards.

Show contributing sources:

- questionnaire items
- stress-context items
- observer familiarity
- chart alignment
- contradictions
- uncertainty responses

Use categories:

- strongly supported
- moderately supported
- tentative
- conflicting
- insufficient information

Explain why confidence is limited.

Example:

> Tentative because only two relevant situations were observed and the answers changed by relationship context.

## Graph 10 — Growth Leverage Matrix

### Question answered

Which changes are likely to create the greatest practical improvement?

### Recommended form

A 2×2 matrix:

- horizontal: effort or difficulty
- vertical: likely practical impact

Place 3–6 recommendations into:

- quick wins
- high-value practices
- supportive experiments
- lower-priority changes

Do not claim predicted treatment outcomes. These are practical experiments derived from the pattern profile.

Each recommendation should have:

- evidence link
- confidence
- suggested trial period
- observable success signal

## Graph 11 — Environment Fit Dashboard

### Question answered

Under what environmental conditions is the person most likely to function well?

Possible dimensions:

- structure ↔ freedom
- solitude ↔ stimulation
- stable routine ↔ frequent novelty
- collaboration ↔ independent ownership
- private contribution ↔ visible recognition
- gradual transition ↔ rapid change

This is not career destiny. It is an environmental-fit hypothesis.

## Graph 12 — “How to Work With Me” Share Card

### Purpose

Create a concise, useful, shareable artifact without exposing sensitive details.

Sections:

- I work best when…
- When I am stressed, you may notice…
- The best way to give me feedback is…
- Please do not assume…
- What helps me reset…
- A strength I bring…

Users must control what is included before sharing.

## Optional advanced visuals

These belong after V1 stability:

- self-versus-observer comparison
- partner comparison
- profile change over repeated assessments
- relationship interaction map
- construct confidence over time
- retrospective pattern timeline

## Visualization data contract

Each visual component should receive structured data similar to:

```ts
interface VisualizationDatum {
  constructId: string;
  label: string;
  baselineBand?: "low" | "moderate" | "high";
  stressBand?: "low" | "moderate" | "high";
  internalScore?: number;
  confidence: "strong" | "moderate" | "tentative" | "conflicting" | "insufficient";
  evidenceCount: number;
  contradictionCount: number;
  chartAlignment?: "supported" | "partial" | "contextual" | "contradicted" | "unresolved";
  explanationId: string;
  assessmentVersion: string;
}
```

The UI should not calculate psychological scores. It should render result objects produced by the engine.

## Accessibility requirements

Every graph needs:

- descriptive title
- one-sentence answer to the graph’s question
- keyboard focus order
- screen-reader text
- data table or structured text alternative
- non-color markers
- readable labels at 320px width
- reduced-motion behavior

## Mobile interaction rules

- one primary graph per screen section
- no tiny axis text
- allow horizontal detail cards rather than forcing dense desktop charts
- use tap-to-explain interactions
- preserve the summary while deeper details expand
- avoid pinch-only controls

## Visual language

The visual design should feel:

- premium
- calm
- intelligent
- personal
- emotionally resonant
- slightly mysterious without becoming occult or unscientific

Avoid:

- clinical hospital styling
- childish quiz styling
- horoscope clichés
- bright red danger scores
- gamified “good personality” points
- visual complexity that disguises weak meaning

## Export requirements

Support:

- accessible HTML report
- print-friendly report
- PDF export later or during V1 if stable
- JSON export of structured results
- user-selected share card

The exported report must include assessment and scoring version, completion date, limitations, and deletion/privacy information.
