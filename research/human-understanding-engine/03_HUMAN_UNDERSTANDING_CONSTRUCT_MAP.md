# Human Understanding Construct Map

## Purpose

The construct map defines what the engine is attempting to understand. It prevents the quiz from becoming a random collection of insightful-sounding questions.

Each construct must eventually have:

- a scientific definition
- a plain-language definition
- facets
- observable behaviors
- baseline expression
- stress expression
- developmental considerations
- cultural considerations
- confounds
- item coverage
- scoring rules
- chart-hypothesis mappings
- interpretation rules
- action implications
- evidence grade
- validation status

## V1 construct set

### HUE-01 — Social Energy and Engagement

**Question:** How does the person seek, use, and recover from social contact?

Facets:

- approach toward people
- stimulation preference
- expressiveness
- social initiative
- recovery need
- depth versus breadth of connection

Do not reduce this to introvert versus extrovert. A person may be socially skilled but easily depleted, or socially cautious but deeply connection-seeking.

Baseline evidence examples:

- initiates plans
- thinks aloud versus privately
- enjoys attention versus prefers observation
- chooses group versus one-to-one interaction
- requires solitude after social demands

Stress expressions:

- withdrawal
- excessive talking
- approval seeking
- irritability from overstimulation
- isolation that blocks support

Practical output:

- ideal communication setting
- social recovery requirements
- meeting and collaboration guidance

### HUE-02 — Emotional Sensitivity

**Question:** How intensely does the person register emotional, interpersonal, and environmental signals?

Facets:

- criticism sensitivity
- rejection sensitivity
- emotional intensity
- interpersonal cue detection
- sensory sensitivity
- empathic resonance

Sensitivity is not weakness. High sensitivity may support empathy and precision while also increasing overload.

Confounds:

- current anxiety
- trauma exposure
- sleep deprivation
- neurodevelopmental differences
- hostile environment

Practical output:

- feedback delivery
- emotional load management
- signs of overload

### HUE-03 — Emotional Regulation and Recovery

**Question:** What happens after emotion is activated, and how does the person return to equilibrium?

Facets:

- impulse control
- naming and communicating emotion
- suppression
- rumination
- reappraisal
- recovery speed
- support seeking

Separate intensity from regulation. A person can feel intensely and regulate well, or feel moderately and remain dysregulated for a long time.

Stress expressions:

- escalation
- shutdown
- rumination
- avoidance
- emotional leakage
- rapid repair

Practical output:

- best timing for difficult conversations
- recovery strategies
- repair guidance

### HUE-04 — Autonomy and Control Needs

**Question:** How strongly does the person need choice, ownership, independence, and freedom from control?

Facets:

- decision ownership
- resistance to pressure
- preference for self-direction
- willingness to ask for help
- response to authority
- boundary assertion

Potential tension:

- wants freedom but also wants reassurance
- asks for advice but resists instruction
- appears compliant until choice feels permanently removed

Practical output:

- how to offer options
- how to avoid triggering reactance
- how to create accountability without domination

### HUE-05 — Belonging, Attachment, and Reassurance

**Question:** How does the person seek connection, safety, closeness, and evidence that relationships are secure?

Facets:

- closeness preference
- reassurance seeking
- abandonment sensitivity
- trust formation
- comfort receiving care
- comfort depending on others

Do not diagnose attachment style from V1. Describe observable needs and patterns.

Practical output:

- connection signals
- reassurance versus space
- trust-building behaviors

### HUE-06 — Response to Criticism and Evaluation

**Question:** How does the person interpret and respond to correction, judgment, failure, or public evaluation?

Facets:

- shame activation
- defensiveness
- curiosity
- perfectionism
- public versus private feedback response
- recovery after mistakes

Stress expressions:

- argument
- collapse
- over-explaining
- avoidance
- counterattack
- obsessive correction

Practical output:

- feedback format
- timing and privacy
- language that preserves learning and dignity

### HUE-07 — Decision-Making Style

**Question:** How does the person gather information, evaluate options, and commit?

Facets:

- intuitive versus deliberative processing
- information-seeking
- speed
- consultation
- reversibility preference
- risk analysis
- post-decision rumination

Potential tensions:

- appears decisive but privately doubts
- researches extensively but decides emotionally
- seeks consensus but resents influence

Practical output:

- ideal decision process
- common traps
- how others can support without taking over

### HUE-08 — Conflict and Boundary Style

**Question:** How does the person respond when needs, values, expectations, or power collide?

Facets:

- avoidance
- accommodation
- directness
- escalation
- boundary clarity
- repair attempts
- fairness orientation

Separate ordinary disagreement from prolonged threat or abuse.

Practical output:

- escalation pattern
- early warning signs
- repair sequence
- boundary language

### HUE-09 — Need for Certainty and Ambiguity Tolerance

**Question:** How does the person respond when outcomes, rules, intentions, or identities are unclear?

Facets:

- uncertainty tolerance
- planning need
- reassurance need
- black-and-white interpretation
- flexibility
- information closure

Confounds:

- immediate danger
- financial instability
- medical uncertainty
- developmental stage
- unfamiliar environment

Practical output:

- level of structure needed
- how to communicate unknowns
- when planning becomes rigidity

### HUE-10 — Motivation and Reward Orientation

**Question:** What most reliably activates sustained effort?

Potential motives:

- mastery
- achievement
- recognition
- belonging
- contribution
- autonomy
- security
- novelty
- competition
- meaning

Do not force one permanent motive. Motivation may differ by role and context.

Practical output:

- effective encouragement
- demotivators
- sustainable reward patterns
- mismatch between stated and observed motives

### HUE-11 — Adaptability and Change Response

**Question:** How quickly and effectively does the person update when plans, roles, environments, or expectations change?

Facets:

- novelty seeking
- transition speed
- routine dependence
- cognitive flexibility
- identity flexibility
- improvisation

Stress expressions:

- rigidity
- impulsive reinvention
- paralysis
- over-control
- excitement that ignores consequences

Practical output:

- preparation needs
- transition pacing
- support during change

### HUE-12 — Stress Expression and Coping

**Question:** What predictable pattern emerges when demands exceed perceived resources?

Possible patterns:

- fight
- flight
- freeze
- appease
- control
- isolate
- over-function
- seek reassurance
- distract
- problem-solve

This construct integrates but does not replace the other eleven. Stress behavior should be linked to the underlying need or threat.

Practical output:

- early warning signs
- unhelpful coping loops
- recovery conditions
- what others should and should not do

## Construct interaction pairs

The report should analyze selected interactions rather than showing isolated scores only.

High-value V1 interactions:

- autonomy × reassurance
- sensitivity × regulation
- certainty need × adaptability
- social energy × recovery need
- criticism sensitivity × perfectionism indicators
- directness × conflict avoidance
- motivation × environmental fit
- stress intensity × recovery speed

Interaction claims must be explicitly coded and tested. Do not let AI freely invent interactions from score combinations.
